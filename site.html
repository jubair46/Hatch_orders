"Order at any counter. The food finds its way to your table, not the other way around."

Hatch started as a single hot plate at a Tuesday market — six cooks who'd worked kitchens in Beirut, Kochi, Kyoto, Chengdu, Oaxaca, and Naples, sharing one gas line because none of them could afford their own. When the stall got too small, they didn't merge their menus. They built a room in Bengaluru with six counters instead.

That's still how it runs. Each kitchen keeps its own recipes, its own spice case, its own rules about what's non-negotiable. What changed is that you can now sit down while you wait — and finish, if you like, at the one counter all six of them share.
